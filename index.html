It is not a guide for clinical protocols or AEC set-up. Any change to clinical settings must be reviewed by a team: breast radiologist, radiographer, medical physicist and, where needed, the manufacturer's application specialist. What the last change did, and why

Definitions used in this lab

  • Mean glandular dose (MGD): the average dose to the glandular tissue of one breast for one exposure. It is calculated from the air kerma at the breast surface and conversion factors that depend on thickness, glandularity and beam quality. The gauge compares it with the European acceptable and achievable levels for the same thickness. These levels are defined for a standard phantom, so real breasts with dense regions are often somewhat above the phantom values.
  • Automatic exposure control (AEC): a short pre-exposure finds the densest region of the breast (about 4 × 4 cm) and the unit sets the mAs, and in the fully automatic mode also kV and filter, so that this region reaches the planned detector signal. The detector signal in % tells you whether the exposure was right; the brightness of the processed image does not.
  • Scatter: photons that changed direction in the breast carry no image information and lower contrast. The scatter-to-primary ratio leaving the breast grows with thickness and field size and hardly depends on kV. A grid or an air gap removes most of it; the scatter fraction is what remains in the detector signal. A small part of the scatter leaves the unit into the room: the operator is protected by the screen.
  • Sharpness: MTF50 is the spatial frequency at which the system keeps half of the contrast of fine detail. It combines the detector pixel, the focal spot (geometric unsharpness Ug = focal spot × (M − 1)) and motion during the exposure.
  • Detectability d′: how reliably the selected lesion can be found, from its contrast, its size, the image noise and the overlapping tissue. Below 2 the lesion is likely to be missed; above about 5 it is easy. Calcifications are limited by noise and sharpness, masses by overlapping dense tissue.
  • The images are synthetic and the numbers come from a simplified model of generic modern equipment. Real units differ between vendors.

Definitions used in this lab

  • X-ray spectra: Kramers bremsstrahlung with the K-lines of Mo and Rh, filtered by the selected filter (30 µm Mo, 25 µm Rh, 50 µm Rh or Ag, 0.7 mm Al), the tube window and the compression paddle. Inherent filtration was fitted so that the HVLs match published measurements with the paddle (NHSBSP 1407) within ±0.02 mm Al. Output is 38 µGy/mAs at 1 m for Mo/Mo 28 kV. For W anodes the output is scaled to match, within 3%, the mean of two NHSBSP technical evaluations (W/Rh: 9.4, 13.0, 16.5 and 20.1 µGy/mAs at 25, 28, 31 and 34 kV). Attenuation uses NIST/Elam coefficients, energy by energy.
  • Breast model: compressed thickness 21–90 mm (equivalent to 20–70 mm PMMA), a 1.5 mm skin layer on each side, rounded uncompressed edge, and a fibroglandular texture with a power-law spectrum (β ≈ 3) concentrated in the central and subareolar region. "Typical" glandularity follows Dance et al. (2000) for women aged 50–64 (97% at 21 mm to 4% at 90 mm).
  • AEC: a pre-exposure finds the densest 4 × 4 cm region (or uses the fixed region selected) and sets mAs so that the detector signal there reaches the target, ±15% per density step. The target rises by 0.8% per mm above 60 mm to hold the CNR. Automatic modes also choose kV and target/filter from the indicated thickness and the density. The tube current is the maximum for the focal spot; the back-up timer stops at 4 s or 600 mAs. With these settings the model gives 0.41, 0.62, 0.93, 1.11, 1.37, 2.1 and 2.3 mGy for 20–70 mm PMMA (a-Se, W anode), between the two published systems used for calibration.
  • Scatter and grid: the scatter-to-primary ratio without grid follows Boone et al. (2000), SPR = [a + b·D−1.5 + c·T−0.5]−1, within 3% for the full field (0.23 at 21 mm to 1.11 at 90 mm), and is spread by a broad kernel so that collimation reduces it. Linear 5:1 grid: primary transmission 0.72, scatter 0.20; cellular grid: 0.80 / 0.12. Magnification uses an air gap instead of a grid. In DBT no grid is used and the reconstruction filter removes about 60% of the scatter contrast loss. Room scatter: about 5 × 10⁻⁴ of the incident air kerma at 1 m for an 18 × 24 cm field, scaled with field area (Simpkin 1996, as used in NCRP 147).
  • Image quality: NEQ from the spectrum at the detector, detector absorption (a-Se 200 µm, CsI 150 µm, CR plate), Swank factor, electronic and structure noise. MTF = detector (pixel aperture and blur; system MTF50 about 5.9 lp/mm for a-Se, 3.4 for CsI, 2.8 for CR) × focal spot (Ug = f × (M − 1)) × motion (0.03 mm/s, ×6 if restless) × DBT blur. d′: non-prewhitening observer (50% efficiency) with quantum noise and anatomical noise that grows with glandularity and thickness; DBT reduces anatomical noise to 38% (15°) or 24% (50°).
  • Threshold gold thickness: thinnest gold disc of a CDMAM-type phantom detected at each diameter, from the same observer model with the lower efficiency of human readers for the smallest discs. The model is set to about 0.8 µm at 0.1 mm and 0.18 µm at 0.25 mm for the a-Se detector at its AEC dose for 50 mm PMMA; published values for such systems are 0.6–1.0 and 0.15–0.24 µm. European limits: 1.68 / 0.352 µm acceptable and 1.10 / 0.244 µm achievable.
  • Mean glandular dose: MGD = K × g × c × s (× T for DBT), with K the incident air kerma at the upper breast surface (no backscatter) and g, c, s from Dance et al. (1990, 2000, 2009, 2011) as tabulated in the EUREF and NHSBSP protocols. The gauge compares MGD with the EUREF acceptable and achievable levels for the PMMA thickness equivalent to this breast; the same values are used here for DBT. Effective dose ≈ 0.12 × MGD / 2 (breast tissue weighting factor, dose averaged over both breasts); approximate.
  • MGD displayed by the unit: calculated by the unit from its stored tube-output table, the set kV and filter, the indicated thickness and a typical glandularity. It therefore differs from the true MGD when the breast is denser or fattier than typical, and when the tube output, the filter or the thickness readout have drifted.
  • ROI pixel values: raw (For Processing) PV = 40 per µGy of RQA-M2-equivalent detector signal, 14-bit; presentation PV is the processed 12-bit image before the monitor window. Noise SD is measured on the high-pass image (pixel minus its 7 × 7 local mean).
  • QC test bench and faults: tests follow the European protocol (EUREF, 4th edition and supplement) where it gives a limit: kV ±1 kV, HVL above kV/100 + 0.03 mm Al, exposure time below 2 s, MGD below the acceptable level for each PMMA thickness, CNR above the limiting value, threshold gold thickness, uniformity ±15%, ghost factor below 0.3, compression force 130–200 N, thickness readout ±5 mm, chest-wall alignment within 5 mm. Where the protocol compares with a baseline, the "ACTION" levels shown are typical local values. Phantom scoring uses the 4 fibres, 3 speck groups, 3 masses minimum of the RMI 156 type phantom.
  • Values are for generic modern equipment. Real AEC tables, detector targets and DBT designs differ between vendors.
